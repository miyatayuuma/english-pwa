## Corpus impact and disposition

The scan read 560 Read/Cloze items and all 2,478 Vocabulary v3 entries, including canonical surfaces, `answers[]`, and exact active source-occurrence spans. It inspected 5,530 surfaces and 3,081 distinct tokens after the legacy token canonicalizer.

| Candidate generator | Unique pairs | KEEP | REJECT | Notes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Edit distance one | 1,912 | 2 | 1,910 | `buy/by` and `cell/sell` are true acoustic equivalents. |
| Soundex | 5,362 | 8 | 5,354 | `buy/by`, `fair/fare`, `hear/here`, `peace/piece`, `plain/plane`, `principal/principle`, `their/there`, and `weather/whether` are acoustic equivalents in the supported pronunciation variety. |
| Split/fused corpus spans | 10 | 0 | 10 | Every observed concatenation is listed and rejected in the JSON audit. Examples include `area / are a`, `beat / be at`, `insight / in sight`, and `soon / so on`. |

The edit-distance and Soundex sets overlap on 564 pairs (6,710 distinct pairs across both sets). `suite/sweet` and `rain forest/rainforest` are retained because they have the same spoken surface despite different orthography or segmentation; their historical tests are records, not the reason for approval. `prose/pros` and `postwar/post war` are required explicit additions. `to/too` is rejected: `to` has both a strong /tuː/ form and a reduced /tə/ form, while transcript text carries no stress evidence and there is no measured provider-confusion basis for a directional exception. `apart/a part` is rejected because it changes meaning. `one/won` is marked NOT_USED / IRRELEVANT: legacy number canonicalization changes `one` to `1` before the fuzzy candidate stage, so the removed matcher did not rescue that pair.

The retained homophone rules have identical phoneme sequences in the reviewed English pronunciation references; segmentation rules preserve the same spoken word sequence. Cambridge lists `to` with strong `/tuː/` and reduced `/tə/` forms, but lists `too` as `/tuː/` in both UK and US English ([`to`](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/pronunciation/english/to), [`too`](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/pronunciation/english/too)). Since raw transcript text contains no stress evidence, neither direction is registered. Cambridge also lists matching UK and US pronunciations for [`suite`](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/pronunciation/english/suite) and [`sweet`](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/pronunciation/english/sweet), supporting that retained pair.

All non-whitelisted edit-distance and Soundex candidates are classified `REJECT_AS_FALSE_POSITIVE_RISK` in the audit JSON. The full pair inventory, all split/fused spans, and the individual historical/task-specific reviews are retained there for reproducibility.

## Migration decisions

- Keep as explicit rules: `prose/pros`, `postwar/post war`, `rain forest/rainforest`, `suite/sweet`, `hear/here`, `buy/by`, `cell/sell`, `fair/fare`, `peace/piece`, `plain/plane`, `their/there`, `weather/whether`, and `principal/principle`.
- Reject `to/too` in both directions. Although the strong form of `to` is homophonous with `too`, reduced `/tə/` is phonemically distinct; without prosody or provider-specific evidence, a surface-only direction cannot establish which form was spoken.
- Remove generic one-edit, Soundex, and unrestricted fusion authority. Similar spellings and concatenation coincidences do not receive credit.
- Do not register task-negative examples (`cat/cut`, `live/love`, `price/prize`, `walk/talk`, `yell/yeah`, or `apart/a part`).
- Cloze target misses make that Cloze attempt fail, suppress no-hint success, and cap its candidate at the prior level so a high overall F1 cannot promote it. The Read F1 thresholds and level/SRS implementation are unchanged.
- Replace the old fixture with `strict-alignment-v1` expectations. The old 42-case snapshot remains evidence about historical behavior, not the correctness standard.
